#### Progress
- **What I accomplished**:
  - Developed three possible game concepts: Cat Cafe Chaos, Cozy Garden, and Lost & Found.
  - Created pitch cards for each concept and compared their recurring decisions, goals, and prototype possibilities.
  - Selected **Cat Cafe Chaos** as the main concept because it has a clear recurring decision and can be tested with a small physical prototype.
  - Created a one-page treatment describing the game's premise, experience, gameplay loop, goal, pressure, and scope.
  - Identified the main design question: how many simultaneous customer and cat needs the player can manage before the game becomes too stressful.
  - Planned a first physical prototype using a café layout, request cards, task cards, happiness or patience values, and a short timer or turn system.

- **Challenges faced**:
  - One challenge was deciding which concept would have the clearest gameplay loop while still being feasible to prototype.
  - Another challenge was keeping Cat Cafe Chaos small enough to test the main mechanic without adding unnecessary features.

- **Solutions**:
  - I compared the concepts based on their recurring player decisions, potential gameplay pressure, and how easily their main mechanics could be represented with physical materials.
  - For Cat Cafe Chaos, I decided to focus on prioritizing customer and cat needs instead of developing advanced cat behavior, upgrades, decorations, or a larger café management system.

#### Learnings
- I learned that narrowing a game concept to its most important mechanic makes it easier to identify what should be tested first.
- I also learned that a concept can sound fun but still need to be evaluated based on whether its core decision is clear and feasible to prototype.
- For Cat Cafe Chaos, the main gameplay decision is choosing which customer or cat need to handle first when multiple requests happen at the same time.

#### Free Thinking
- I am interested in making the cats create unpredictable situations so the game feels playful and slightly chaotic instead of being only a basic task-management game.
- I could experiment with different numbers of simultaneous requests to see when the game becomes challenging versus overwhelming.
- I also want to determine whether different tasks should take different amounts of time or have different effects on customer and cat happiness.

#### Next Steps
- Create the first paper/card prototype for Cat Cafe Chaos.
- Test a short café service period using different numbers of customer and cat requests.
- Observe whether prioritizing requests feels interesting or stressful.
- Adjust the number of simultaneous requests, task difficulty, and timing based on the prototype results.
