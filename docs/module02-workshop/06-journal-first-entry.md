# IT265 Module 2: Design and Development Journal First Entry

## Entry Details

**Date:** October 5, 2026

**Working game title:** Cat Cafe Chaos

**Project stage:** Concept development and early prototype planning

**Entry type:** Design decision and feasibility reflection

## Concept and Decision

**Current concept and intended experience:**  
Cat Cafe Chaos is a time-management and simulation game where the player runs a small cat café and must keep both customers and cats happy. The intended experience is busy, playful, and slightly chaotic without becoming overwhelming. The player must respond to different requests, such as serving drinks, cleaning tables, feeding cats, finding missing toys, and petting unhappy cats.

**Design question or decision:**  
The main design question is: **How many simultaneous customer and cat needs can the player manage before the game becomes stressful rather than fun?** I decided that the main focus of the prototype should be prioritizing competing needs rather than trying to build a complete café simulation.

**Selected concept and alternatives kept:**  
I selected Cat Cafe Chaos because it has a clear recurring decision, an immediate source of pressure, and a simple physical prototype that can test the main mechanic. I am keeping **Cozy Garden** and **Lost & Found** as alternative ideas for later.

**Peer feedback that affected the decision, if received (otherwise say unavailable):**  
Unavailable. I did not receive peer feedback for this concept workshop, so I compared the concepts based on their recurring decisions, prototype feasibility, and potential design risks.

## Reading Connection

**Section and specific idea:**  
**Feasibility** — an idea should be reduced to a manageable version so that its important design questions can be tested before developing a larger experience.

**Relevance today:** Still relevant

**Reason and supporting example:**  
This idea is relevant because Cat Cafe Chaos could easily become too large if I tried to include advanced cat behavior, café upgrades, decorations, a large menu, and many different customer types. Instead, I can create a small physical prototype focused only on customer and cat requests, task completion, and happiness or patience values. This makes the main mechanic easier to test.

**Connection to a choice, question or proposed test for my game:**  
This influenced my decision to focus the first prototype on prioritization. I want to test whether deciding which customer or cat to help first creates meaningful choices. The prototype will also help determine how many simultaneous requests the player can handle before the experience becomes overwhelming.

## Progress and Evidence

**What I created, changed, tested, or decided:**  
I developed the Cat Cafe Chaos concept and identified the main tasks the player may perform: serving drinks, cleaning tables, feeding cats, finding missing toys, and petting unhappy cats. I also decided to use customer and cat request cards, task cards, happiness or patience values, and a short timer or turn system for the first physical prototype.

**Direct artifact link or specific observation:**  
The concept, pitch card, selection and scope notes, and one-page treatment describe the same core loop: the player notices multiple needs, decides which one to handle first, completes a task, and responds to the next request.

**What the evidence confirms:**  
The concept has a clear recurring decision and can be represented with simple physical materials. The main source of gameplay pressure is also clear: customers and cats can need attention at the same time.

**What remains uncertain:**  
I have not tested the prototype yet, so I do not know how many simultaneous requests will create interesting decision-making instead of frustration. I also need to determine whether the different tasks feel meaningfully different or simply become repetitive.

## Rough Gameplay Timing Onion

| Layer | Player activity or outcome | Tentative timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate decision | Decide which customer or cat request to handle first. | About 10–30 seconds |
| Larger objective | Complete several tasks while keeping customers and cats from becoming too unhappy. | About 1–3 minutes |
| Session | Successfully manage a short café service period before too many customers or cats become unhappy. | About 5–10 minutes |

These timings are estimates and have not been tested yet.

Create a paper/card prototype with a simple café layout, customer and cat request cards, task cards, happiness or patience values, and a short timer or turn system. Then play through a short service period to test different numbers of simultaneous requests.

**Uncertainty it will address:**  
The prototype will help determine whether prioritizing customer and cat needs is fun and understandable, and how much simultaneous pressure the player can handle before the game becomes too stressful.
