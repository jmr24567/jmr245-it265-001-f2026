IT265 Module 2: Concept Pitch Cards

Copy this card for each of your two or three shortlisted concepts. Aim to explain each in about one minute. Use your idea bank as a starting point.

Pitch Card 1

Working title: Cat Cafe Chaos

Player role and situation:
The player runs a small cat café and is responsible for keeping both the customers and cats happy. Customers need service while cats can create unexpected problems that require attention.

Repeated decision or action:
The player decides which customer or cat need to handle first and completes tasks such as serving drinks, cleaning tables, feeding cats, finding missing toys, and petting unhappy cats.

Goal, pressure, or ending:
The goal is to keep enough customers and cats happy throughout the café's service period. The pressure comes from having multiple requests at the same time. The session ends when the service period is successfully completed or too many customers and cats become unhappy.

Hook:
Unlike a normal café simulation, the cats can create unpredictable problems while the player is already trying to serve customers, creating a playful sense of chaos.

Smallest useful physical prototype:
A paper prototype using a simple café layout, customer and cat request cards, task cards, and a short timer or turn system.

Question I want listeners to answer:
Does choosing between customer needs and cat needs sound like an interesting decision, or would the game become too stressful?

Pitch Card 2

Working title: Cozy Garden

Player role and situation:
The player takes care of a small garden and is responsible for helping different plants grow successfully.

Repeated decision or action:
The player decides which plants need attention and chooses whether to water them, move them toward sunlight, or take care of another garden task.

Goal, pressure, or ending:
The goal is to grow a healthy garden. The player has limited time or resources, so they must decide which plants need attention first. The session ends when the garden reaches a growth goal.

Hook:
The garden changes based on the player's choices, creating a relaxing experience where the player gradually sees their small garden develop.

Smallest useful physical prototype:
A paper garden board with plant cards, resource tokens, and action cards representing watering, sunlight, and plant care.

Question I want listeners to answer:
Would the gardening decisions be interesting enough to keep the game engaging while still feeling relaxing?

Pitch Card 3

Working title: Lost & Found

Player role and situation:
The player works at a lost-and-found area and helps people recover objects they have misplaced.

Repeated decision or action:
The player searches for objects, identifies clues about their owners, and decides where each item should be placed or who it should be returned to.

Goal, pressure, or ending:
The goal is to correctly return as many lost objects as possible. The player may have limited time and must decide which objects or clues to investigate first. The session ends when the available cases are completed or time runs out.

Hook:
Each lost object can provide clues about its owner, turning a simple search into a small mystery.

Smallest useful physical prototype:
A collection of object cards, owner cards, clue cards, and a simple board representing the lost-and-found area.

Question I want listeners to answer:
Does matching objects with their owners create enough of a puzzle to make the search process interesting?
